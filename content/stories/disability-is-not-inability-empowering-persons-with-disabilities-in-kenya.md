PWDs in Kenya often face exclusion in education, employment, and digital opportunities. Many barriers are systemic, including limited accessibility, societal stigma, and lack of skills training. Mercy Wanjau Foundation’s PWD pillar seeks to dismantle these obstacles by providing tools, mentorship, and inclusive programs that enable PWDs to thrive.

> Disability is not a brave struggle or ‘courage in the face of adversity.’ Disability is an art. It’s an ingenious way to live.
>
> — Neil Marcus

## 1. Bridging the Digital Divide

Technology offers unprecedented opportunities for PWDs, from remote work to digital entrepreneurship. MWF provides:

- Digital literacy training
- Assistive technology tools
- Mentorship in online platforms
- Remote work preparation

By equipping PWDs with these skills, the Foundation ensures they can access income opportunities and participate fully in the digital economy.

### 2. Inclusive Education and Skills Development

Education and skill-building are critical. PWD programs include:

- Specialized digital skills courses
- Vocational and business training
- Adapted learning materials
- Peer-to-peer mentorship

These interventions build confidence, independence, and long-term economic sustainability.

### 3. Overcoming Societal Stigma

Stigma and discrimination often prevent PWDs from achieving their potential. MWF organizes community awareness campaigns to highlight the capabilities of PWDs and promote inclusion in schools, workplaces, and public spaces.

## How You Can Help

- Sponsor PWD digital skills cohorts or training sessions
- Provide assistive technology tools (adaptive devices, software)
- Volunteer as a mentor or skills trainer
- Advocate for inclusive employment policies

Supporting these programs ensures PWDs can access opportunities and live independently.

## Conclusion

Disability is not inability. Mercy Wanjau Foundation empowers PWDs to build digital, professional, and personal independence. Your support—through mentorship, resources, or advocacy—directly transforms lives and fosters inclusive communities in Kenya.
