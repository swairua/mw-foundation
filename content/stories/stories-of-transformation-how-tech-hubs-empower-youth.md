Behind every statistic about Kenya’s digital divide lies a human story. For many young people, access to technology is the difference between stagnation and transformation. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation’s tech hubs are designed to unlock these stories of change.

Take the example of a young student in Kirinyaga County who had never touched a computer before entering one of our hubs. Within months, she learned basic digital literacy, progressed to coding, and eventually built a small website for her family’s business. What began as curiosity became confidence, and confidence became opportunity.

Tech hubs are more than classrooms. They are incubators of innovation and creativity. Youth gather to learn, collaborate, and dream. They explore fields like graphic design, digital marketing, and app development. They discover that technology is not just about consumption but creation. And in the process, they begin to see themselves as contributors to Kenya’s digital economy.

## Why we need your support

Volunteers play a critical role in this transformation. Mentors guide learners through challenges, share real-world insights, and inspire confidence. Corporate partners provide resources and training, ensuring sustainability. Donors make it possible to expand hubs into more counties, reaching more youth.

> Technology is best when it brings people together.
>
> — Matt Mullenweg, co-founder of WordPress

The ripple effect is powerful. A single youth empowered through technology can uplift an entire family. A group of digitally skilled youth can transform a community. By equipping young people with tools and skills, the Foundation is planting seeds of resilience and growth.

Every story of transformation is a reminder that technology is not just about devices it is about people. It is about unlocking potential, fostering dignity, and creating pathways to empowerment. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation’s tech hubs are beacons of hope, lighting the way for Kenya’s youth to thrive in the digital age.
