Stigma surrounding mental health prevents many people in Kenya from seeking help. Misconceptions, fear of judgment, and cultural barriers often lead individuals to hide their struggles. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation (MWF) addresses these issues through mental health awareness campaigns, training sessions, and community engagement that normalize conversations about emotional well-being.

> Mental health… is not a destination, but a process. It’s about how you drive, not where you’re going.
>
> — Noam Shpancer, PhD**

### Understanding the Impact of Stigma

Stigma isolates individuals and creates barriers to accessing care. People may feel ashamed or misunderstood, resulting in untreated depression, anxiety, or trauma. Educating communities about mental health challenges helps reduce judgment, encourages early intervention, and promotes empathy.

### Training and Awareness Programs

MWF runs monthly online and in-person sessions that teach:

- Recognizing mental health challenges
- Effective communication and supportive listening
- Self-care strategies
- Referral pathways to professionals

By training local leaders, volunteers, and educators, MWF creates a network of individuals capable of promoting mental wellness within their communities.

### Community Engagement and Peer Support

Peer-support circles and discussion groups allow individuals to share experiences in a safe environment. This reduces feelings of isolation, encourages collaboration, and promotes practical problem-solving. Participants often report improved confidence in helping themselves and others.

## How You Can Help

- Volunteer as a facilitator or mentor
- Sponsor community mental health awareness programs
- Help develop culturally relevant educational materials
- Advocate for mental health initiatives in schools and churches

Your support empowers communities to tackle stigma head-on.

## Conclusion

Breaking mental health stigma is vital for building resilient communities. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation’s efforts in awareness, training, and peer support provide individuals with the knowledge, skills, and confidence to prioritize mental wellness. Every volunteer, sponsor, or advocate strengthens this important work.
