Inclusivity sits at the heart of equitable education. Yet for many learners with disabilities in Kenya, access to quality education remains limited. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation is committed to changing this narrative through its Education & Leadership pillar by advocating for inclusive learning spaces, supporting schools with resources, and ensuring that every child regardless of ability has the opportunity to learn, lead, and thrive.

## The Barriers Facing Learners With Disabilities

Children with disabilities often face multiple challenges, including:

- Lack of accessible learning materials
- Stigma and discrimination
- Inadequate teacher training
- Poor infrastructure
- Limited assistive devices
- Isolation from mainstream classrooms

These factors cause many to drop out or perform poorly in school.

We believe that **every child can learn when given the right support**.

> The highest result of education is tolerance.
>
> — Helen Keller

## What the Foundation Is Doing to Promote Inclusion

### 1. Inclusive School Libraries

The Foundation ensures that libraries include:

- Braille books
- Large-print materials
- Quiet reading zones
- Accessible furniture
- Visual learning aids

### 2. Teacher Training

Teachers receive guidance on:

- Inclusive teaching methods
- Handling diverse learning needs
- Using assistive tools
- Creating emotionally safe classrooms

### 3. The French Initiative’s Adaptation

Children with disabilities actively participate in French classes with tailored teaching strategies to ensure no one is left behind.

## How You Can Support Inclusive Education

- Donate braille books or devices
- Fund accessible infrastructure
- Provide teacher training support
- Volunteer as a literacy or inclusion specialist
- Partner for disability-focused programs

## Conclusion

Inclusive education builds dignity, hope, and equality. By supporting learners with disabilities, we support a stronger, more compassionate Kenya.
