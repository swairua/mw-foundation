Empowering women and girls through education is not just a moral imperative it is one of the most effective ways to promote social and economic development in Kenya. When girls learn, communities become healthier, families become more financially stable, and nations become stronger. Yet millions of girls still face barriers that prevent them from accessing the education they deserve.

At the Mercy Wanjau Foundation, the Education & Leadership pillar is deeply committed to ensuring that girls and women from underserved communities have access to quality education, leadership opportunities, safe learning spaces, and the support systems needed to grow. Empowering a single girl has a ripple effect that transforms entire families and communities.

## Why Girls’ Education Matters

Empowering girls through education yields lifelong benefits:

- **Increased earning potential:** Educated women earn higher salaries and contribute more to their families’ economic stability.
- **Lower early marriage rates:** Girls who stay in school are less likely to marry early.
- **Better health outcomes:** Educated mothers raise healthier children and make informed health decisions.
- **Leadership potential:** Girls with education step into leadership roles, both locally and nationally.
- **Intergenerational impact:** Educated women ensure their own children attend school and value learning.

Education has the power to break cycles of poverty and inequality but only when girls are given equal access.

## Challenges Kenyan Girls Face

Despite progress, many girls in Kenya continue to face significant barriers:

- **Poverty and lack of school fees**
- **Early or forced marriage**
- **Gender-based violence and harassment**
- **Cultural biases favoring boys’ education**
- **Lack of menstrual hygiene resources**
- **Long walking distances to school**

These obstacles often result in girls dropping out of school or performing poorly because they lack the necessary support and resources.

The Mercy Wanjau Foundation aims to confront these challenges head-on through targeted interventions that prioritize inclusion, safety, mentorship, and empowerment.

## What the Foundation Is Doing to Empower Girls

## 1. Scholarships and Financial Assistance

The Foundation provides scholarships to girls from vulnerable households, ensuring that financial constraints do not stand in the way of their education. These scholarships cover tuition, uniforms, learning materials, and sanitary products.

## 2. Leadership & Mentorship Programs

Girls participate in leadership workshops designed to build self-esteem, communication skills, and confidence. Mentorship from successful Kenyan women helps guide them in developing career goals and life skills.

## 3. Safe Learning Spaces

Through our school library initiative, the Foundation prioritizes creating safe, inclusive learning environments where girls feel supported and valued.

## 4. French Initiative Access for Girls

Girls involved in The French Initiative gain a competitive advantage in global careers by learning a new language and expanding their academic horizons
