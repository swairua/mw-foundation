For persons with disabilities, technology is more than convenience — it is liberation. Yet across Kenya, many remain excluded from the digital economy due to lack of access to assistive devices. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation’s Technology Pillar is committed to changing this reality.

Assistive devices such as screen readers, adaptive keyboards, and specialized software open doors to independence. They enable persons with disabilities to access education, secure employment, and participate fully in society. Remote work opportunities, in particular, offer dignity and flexibility, allowing individuals to contribute without barriers.

The Foundation partners with specialized institutions to procure and distribute these devices. But the impact goes beyond hardware. Training programs ensure that beneficiaries can use the tools effectively, building confidence and competence. Mentorship connects them with role models who demonstrate that disability is not inability.

Support from donors and partners is vital. Contributions fund procurement of devices, training materials, and outreach programs. Corporate sponsors can provide technical expertise, while volunteers can mentor beneficiaries. Together, we can ensure that persons with disabilities are not left behind in Kenya’s digital future.

> Making this world accessible is nothing but removing the barriers placed by the society.
>
> — Dr. Kalyan C. Kankanala

Assistive technology is not charity — it is justice. It affirms the right of every individual to participate fully in society. The Mercy Wanjau Foundation stands as a beacon of hope, illuminating pathways to empowerment for persons with disabilities through technology.
